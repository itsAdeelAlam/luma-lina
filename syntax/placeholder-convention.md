# Placeholder Convention

**Status:** Official

## Purpose

Square brackets are used to mark words, names, or expressions that are temporary placeholders during the development of **Luma Lina**.

A placeholder represents a concept that is needed in an example or explanation, but whose final form has not yet been established.

## Basic Rule

Temporary English words used as stand-ins for future Luma Lina words are enclosed in square brackets.

Example:

```text
Ei la [teacher].

Ao la [student].
```

Here, `[teacher]` and `[student]` represent concepts whose Luma Lina words have not yet been chosen.

The English words inside the brackets are **not official vocabulary**.

## Status, Not Origin

Brackets indicate the **status** of a word, not its origin or appearance.

A word does not need brackets simply because it resembles an English word or originated from another language.

If a word has been accepted as an official word of Luma Lina, it is written without brackets.

For example:

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

`buff` is an official Luma Lina word, so it is not written as `[buff]`.

Likewise, established words such as `yum` and `maimai` are written without brackets.

## Personal Names

Actual personal names are not treated as placeholders and are written without brackets.

Example:

```text
Ei la Adeel.
```

`Adeel` is a personal name, so it does not require brackets.

A temporary invented name or unnamed person may use brackets when necessary.

Example:

```text
[Person A] la [teacher].
```

## Becoming Official

A placeholder becomes an official word only when a final form is explicitly accepted.

The general development process is:

```text
[teacher]

    ↓

A final Luma Lina form is chosen

    ↓

The form is explicitly accepted

    ↓

The word is added to the official vocabulary

    ↓

[teacher] is replaced by the accepted word
```

Once the final word is accepted, the brackets are removed.

A word does not become official simply because it has appeared repeatedly in examples or documents.

## Multi-Word Placeholders

When a placeholder represents a complete phrase or multiple-word expression, use one pair of brackets around the entire placeholder.

Correct:

```text
[the day after tomorrow]
```

Not:

```text
[the] [day] [after] [tomorrow]
```

One pair of brackets represents one unfinished element.

## Language Name

`Luma Lina` is the established name of the language.

The name is official and is therefore written **without brackets**.

Example:

```text
Luma Lina
```

The placeholder convention does not apply to the language name because its status has already been finalized.

## Placeholder Examples

### Temporary vocabulary

```text
Ei la [teacher].

Ao la [student].
```

### Official vocabulary

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

`buff` is an official word and therefore has no brackets.

### Personal name

```text
Ei la Adeel.
```

`Adeel` is an actual personal name and therefore has no brackets.

### Temporary phrase

```text
Ei [go] [to the old market].
```

Each unfinished element is treated as its own placeholder.

## Square Brackets and Other Linguistic Notation

Square brackets are reserved for placeholders in the current documentation convention.

If Luma Lina later develops formal phonetic or phonological notation, a different notation should be used where appropriate.

For example, `/ /` may be used for phonemic representations if such a system is formally adopted.

This prevents the placeholder convention from conflicting with standard linguistic notation.

## Important Rule

A bracketed form is **not part of the official vocabulary or grammar unless it is explicitly accepted**.

Placeholders are development tools. They allow incomplete examples and explanations to remain readable without pretending that unfinished vocabulary has already been decided.

The brackets disappear when the placeholder is replaced by an accepted form.
