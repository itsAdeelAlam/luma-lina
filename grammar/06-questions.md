# Questions

**Status:** Official

## Overview

Luma Lina has two main types of questions:

1. Yes/no questions
2. Information questions

Both systems use separate grammatical markers.

* `ma` = yes/no question marker
* `Sao` = information-question placeholder

These markers do not change according to person, number, or gender.

---

## Yes/No Questions

Luma Lina uses the marker `ma` to form a yes/no question.

The basic structure is:

```text
Statement + ma?
```

The marker `ma` is placed at the end of the sentence.

For example:

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do I eat meat?

The positive statement is:

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I eat meat.

Adding `ma` turns the statement into a yes/no question:

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

---

## Yes/No Questions with Different Subjects

The same question marker is used with different subjects.

### First Person

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do I eat meat?

### Second Person

```text
Ao yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do you eat meat?

### Third Person

```text
Sa yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Does he/she eat meat?

### Plural Subject

```text
Ei di yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do we eat meat?

```text
Sa di yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do they eat meat?

The verb does not change.

---

## Boolean Responses

Yes/no questions can receive short Boolean responses.

The current response words are:

* `yah` = yes — **Official**
* `na` = no — **Official**

`na` is also the general negation marker used in normal sentences.

Example:

```text
Ao yum buff ma?

Yah.
```

**Meaning:** Do you eat meat?

**Yes.**

Another possible response:

```text
Ao yum buff ma?

Na.
```

**Meaning:** Do you eat meat?

**No.**

Both `yah` and `na` are now **Official** Boolean response words.

No separate Boolean grammar system is required.

---

## Negative Yes/No Questions

The negation marker `na` can be combined with the yes/no question marker `ma`.

The structure is:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate + ma?
```

For example:

```text
Ei na yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Don't I eat meat?

The two markers have separate functions:

* `na` = negation
* `ma` = yes/no question

The order remains predictable:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate + ma?
```

---

## Yes/No Questions with the Copula

The marker `ma` can also be used with the copula `la`.

The structure is:

```text
Subject + la + Complement + ma?
```

Example:

```text
Ei la [teacher] ma?
```

**Meaning:** Am I a teacher?

Another example:

```text
Sa la [teacher] ma?
```

**Meaning:** Is he/she a teacher?

The copula `la` does not change.

---

## Negative Copular Questions

Negation and the copula can also be combined with `ma`.

The structure is:

```text
Subject + na + la + Complement + ma?
```

Example:

```text
Ei na la [teacher] ma?
```

**Meaning:** Am I not a teacher?

The markers remain separate:

* `na` = negation
* `la` = copula
* `ma` = yes/no question

---

## Information Questions

Luma Lina uses `Sao` as an information-question placeholder.

`Sao` represents the missing information being asked for.

Instead of creating separate question words for every type of information, the same placeholder can be used in different sentence positions.

The basic principle is:

```text
Sao = unknown / requested information
```

The surrounding sentence structure shows what type of information is being requested.

---

## Asking for an Object

`Sao` can replace the missing object of a verb.

Example:

```text
Ei yum Sao?
```

**Meaning:** What do I eat?

Structure:

```text
Ei + yum + Sao?

I + eat + what?
```

`Sao` occupies the position where the unknown object would normally appear.

---

## Asking for a Subject

`Sao` can also replace the missing subject.

Example:

```text
Sao yum buff?
```

**Meaning:** Who eats meat?

Structure:

```text
Sao + yum + buff?

who + eat + meat?
```

The same placeholder is used, but its position changes according to the information being requested.

---

## Information Questions and Sentence Structure

Luma Lina keeps the normal sentence structure when possible.

The information-question placeholder `Sao` occupies the position of the missing information.

For example:

```text
Ei yum Sao?
```

can be understood as:

```text
Ei + yum + [unknown object]?
```

The placeholder therefore works compositionally with the existing sentence structure.

---

## Question Mark

Questions are written with a question mark:

```text
?
```

For yes/no questions, `ma` appears before the question mark:

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

For information questions, `Sao` appears in the position of the missing information:

```text
Ei yum Sao?
```

---

## Questions Do Not Change Verb Forms

Questions do not cause verb conjugation.

The verb remains in its normal form.

Compare:

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I eat meat.

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do I eat meat?

The verb `yum` remains unchanged.

---

## Questions with Plural Subjects

Plural subjects continue to use the normal plural marker `di`.

Example:

```text
Ei di yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do we eat meat?

```text
Sa di yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do they eat meat?

The plural marker remains after the word it pluralizes.

---

## Questions and Possession

Possessive phrases use the normal possession/relationship marker `de`.

Example:

```text
Ei yum Ei de maimai ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do I eat my goat?

The possession structure remains:

```text
Ei de maimai
```

**Meaning:** my goat

The question marker `ma` is added at the end of the complete sentence.

---

## Questions and Negation

Negation and questioning combine compositionally.

Example:

```text
Ei na yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Don't I eat meat?

Structure:

```text
Ei + na + yum + buff + ma?

I + not + eat + meat + question
```

Each marker keeps its own function.

---

## Summary

| Type                         | Structure                                         | Example                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Yes/no question              | `Statement + ma?`                                 | `Ei yum buff ma?`        |
| Negative yes/no question     | `Subject + na + Predicate + ma?`                  | `Ei na yum buff ma?`     |
| Copular question             | `Subject + la + Complement + ma?`                 | `Ei la [teacher] ma?`    |
| Negative copular question    | `Subject + na + la + Complement + ma?`            | `Ei na la [teacher] ma?` |
| Information question         | `Sentence with Sao replacing missing information` | `Ei yum Sao?`            |
| Subject information question | `Sao + Predicate + Object?`                       | `Sao yum buff?`          |

### Core Rules

1. `ma` is the official yes/no question marker.
2. `ma` is placed at the end of a yes/no question.
3. `Sao` is the official information-question placeholder.
4. `Sao` replaces the missing information in an information question.
5. `na` remains the general negation marker.
6. `la` remains the invariant copula.
7. `de` remains the possession/relationship marker.
8. `yah` is an **Official** Boolean response meaning “yes.”
9. `na` is an **Official** Boolean response meaning “no.”
10. Questions do not cause verb conjugation.
11. `di` continues to mark plurality normally.
12. Question formation is compositional and does not require separate verb forms.
