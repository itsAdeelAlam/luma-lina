# Demonstratives

**Status:** Official

## Overview

Luma Lina uses demonstratives to show whether something is near or distant.

The basic demonstratives are:

* `ti` = this
* `ta` = that

Demonstratives directly modify the noun they describe.

Plurality is expressed with the existing plural marker `di`, which follows the noun.

This means Luma Lina does not need separate words for:

* these
* those

Instead, the noun is made plural with `di`.

---

## Singular Demonstratives

### `ti`

`ti` means **this** and refers to something near the speaker.

When used with a noun:

```text
ti maimai
```

**Meaning:** this goat

---

### `ta`

`ta` means **that** and refers to something distant from the speaker.

When used with a noun:

```text
ta maimai
```

**Meaning:** that goat

---

## Demonstratives with Plural Nouns

The existing plural marker `di` follows the noun it pluralizes.

The demonstrative remains directly before the noun.

### `ti maimai di`

```text
ti maimai di
```

**Meaning:** these goats

Structure:

```text
ti + maimai + di
this + goat + plural
```

### `ta maimai di`

```text
ta maimai di
```

**Meaning:** those goats

Structure:

```text
ta + maimai + di
that + goat + plural
```

The demonstrative does not receive the plural marker directly.

---

## Demonstrative and Noun Structure

The official structure is:

```text
ti maimai
```

**Meaning:** this goat

```text
ti maimai di
```

**Meaning:** these goats

```text
ta maimai
```

**Meaning:** that goat

```text
ta maimai di
```

**Meaning:** those goats

The pattern is:

```text
Demonstrative + Noun + di
```

when the noun is plural.

The principle remains that `di` follows the word it pluralizes.

Here, `di` follows the noun because the noun is what becomes plural.

---

## Demonstratives as Subjects

A demonstrative can function as the subject of a copular sentence.

### Singular

```text
ti la maimai.
```

**Meaning:** This is a goat.

```text
ta la maimai.
```

**Meaning:** That is a goat.

### Plural

For plural meanings, the noun after the copula is marked with `di`.

```text
ti la maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** These are goats.

```text
ta la maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** Those are goats.

The structure remains:

```text
Subject + la + Complement
```

The noun `maimai` is pluralized by `di`.

---

## Demonstratives with Possession

Demonstratives can be combined with possessive phrases using the existing possession/relationship marker `de`.

Example:

```text
ta maimai la Ei de maimai.
```

**Meaning:** That goat is my goat.

The possession structure remains:

```text
Ei de maimai
```

**Meaning:** my goat

For a plural noun:

```text
ta maimai di la Ei di de maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** Those goats are our goats.

The noun `maimai` is pluralized with `di` because `di` follows the noun it pluralizes.

---

## Demonstratives and Negation

Demonstratives can be used in negative sentences with the general negation marker `na`.

Example:

```text
ta na la maimai.
```

**Meaning:** That is not a goat.

For plural nouns:

```text
ta na la maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** Those are not goats.

The normal negation structure remains:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate
```

---

## Demonstratives in Questions

Demonstratives can be used with the yes/no question marker `ma`.

Example:

```text
ta la maimai ma?
```

**Meaning:** Is that a goat?

For a plural noun:

```text
ta la maimai di ma?
```

**Meaning:** Are those goats?

The question marker `ma` remains at the end of the sentence.

---

## Summary

| Structure      | Meaning     |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| `ti maimai`    | this goat   |
| `ti maimai di` | these goats |
| `ta maimai`    | that goat   |
| `ta maimai di` | those goats |

### Core Rules

1. `ti` means “this” and refers to something near.
2. `ta` means “that” and refers to something distant.
3. `ti` and `ta` directly modify the noun.
4. `di` is used to express plurality.
5. `di` follows the word it pluralizes.
6. When a demonstrative modifies a noun, `di` follows the noun.
7. `ti maimai` means “this goat.”
8. `ti maimai di` means “these goats.”
9. `ta maimai` means “that goat.”
10. `ta maimai di` means “those goats.”
11. `di` does not directly follow `ti` or `ta` in demonstrative+noun phrases.
12. `de` remains the possession/relationship marker.
13. `na` remains the general negation marker.
14. `ma` remains the yes/no question marker.
