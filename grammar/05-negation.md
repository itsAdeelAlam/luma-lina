# Negation

**Status:** Official

## Overview

Luma Lina uses one general negation marker:

`na`

`na` is used to make a sentence negative.

The negation marker does not change according to:

* person
* number
* gender
* tense or aspect

The same form `na` is used with different subjects and predicates.

---

## Basic Negation

The basic structure of negation is:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate
```

For example:

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

The marker `na` comes before the predicate.

---

## Negating a Verb

When the predicate is a verb, `na` is placed before the verb.

Structure:

```text
Subject + na + Verb + Object
```

Example:

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

The verb does not change when negated.

Compare:

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I eat meat.

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

---

## Negating Different Subjects

The same negation marker is used with different pronouns.

### First Person

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

### Second Person

```text
Ao na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** You do not eat meat.

### Third Person

```text
Sa na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** He/she does not eat meat.

### Plural Subject

```text
Ei di na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** We do not eat meat.

```text
Sa di na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** They do not eat meat.

The verb remains unchanged in every example.

---

## Negating the Copula

The negation marker `na` can also be used with the copula `la`.

The structure is:

```text
Subject + na + la + Complement
```

Example:

```text
Ei na la Adeel.
```

**Meaning:** I am not Adeel.

Another example:

```text
Sa na la [teacher].
```

**Meaning:** He/she is not a teacher.

The copula `la` remains unchanged.

---

## Negation with Plural Subjects

Plural subjects use the same negation structure.

```text
Ei di na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** We do not eat meat.

```text
Sa di na la [teacher] di.
```

**Meaning:** They are not teachers.

maimai di na yum buff.

**Meaning:** Goats do not eat meat.

The plural marker `di` remains after the word it pluralizes.

---

## Negation and Possession

Possessive phrases can also appear inside negative sentences.

The possession/relationship marker `de` remains unchanged.

Example:

```text
Ei na yum Ei de maimai.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat my goat.

The structure is:

```text
Ei + na + yum + Ei de maimai
subject + negation + verb + possessive phrase
```

Plural possession follows the same rules:

```text
Ei di na yum Ei di de maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** We do not eat our goats.

---

## Negation with Questions

The yes/no question marker `ma` can be combined with negation.

The basic structure remains:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate + ma?
```

Example:

```text
Ei na yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Don't I eat meat?

The question marker `ma` remains at the end of the sentence.

Negation and questioning therefore use separate grammatical markers:

* `na` = negation
* `ma` = yes/no question

---

## Negation Does Not Change the Verb

Luma Lina does not conjugate verbs when forming negative sentences.

The same verb form is used in positive and negative sentences.

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I eat meat.

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

The verb `yum` remains unchanged.

---

## Core Rule

The general negation structure is:

```text
Subject + na + Predicate
```

`na` is placed before the predicate.

Examples:

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

```text
Ao na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** You do not eat meat.

```text
Sa na la [teacher].
```

**Meaning:** He/she is not a teacher.

```text
Ei di na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** We do not eat meat.

---

## Summary

| Rule                            | Structure                                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| General negation                | `Subject + na + Predicate`                          |
| Verb negation                   | `Subject + na + Verb + Object`                      |
| Copula negation                 | `Subject + na + la + Complement`                    |
| Negative yes/no question        | `Subject + na + Predicate + ma?`                    |
| Possession in negative sentence | `Subject + na + Predicate + Possessor de Possessed` |

### Core Rules

1. `na` is the general negation marker.
2. `na` comes before the predicate.
3. `na` does not change according to person, number, or gender.
4. Verbs do not change when negated.
5. `na` can be used with the copula `la`.
6. `ma` remains the yes/no question marker.
7. `de` remains the possession/relationship marker.
8. Negation combines compositionally with the existing grammar.
