# Word Order

**Status:** Official

## Purpose

This document defines the basic word order of **Luma Lina** and explains how established grammatical elements are arranged within sentences and phrases.

Luma Lina uses a simple and predictable word-order system.

---

## Basic Sentence Order

The default sentence order is:

```text
Subject + Verb + Object
```

This is the basic **SVO** structure.

Example:

```text
Ei yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I eat meat.

Structure:

```text
Ei     yum     buff
Subject Verb    Object
```

The subject comes first, the verb comes second, and the object comes after the verb.

---

## Copular Sentences

Copular sentences use the structure:

```text
Subject + la + Complement
```

The copula `la` connects the subject with a noun, identity, category, or description.

Example:

```text
Ei la Adeel.
```

**Meaning:** I am Adeel.

Another example:

```text
Ei la [teacher].
```

**Meaning:** I am a teacher.

---

## Negation

The negation marker `na` comes before the predicate.

**Structure:**

```text
Subject + na + Predicate
```

Example:

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

**Meaning:** I do not eat meat.

With a copula:

```text
Ei na la Adeel.
```

**Meaning:** I am not Adeel.

---

## Yes/No Questions

The yes/no question marker `ma` comes at the end of the sentence.

**Structure:**

```text
Sentence + ma?
```

Example:

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do I eat meat?

A negative yes/no question keeps the normal negation structure:

```text
Ei na yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Don’t I eat meat?

---

## Information Questions

The information-question placeholder `Sao` appears in the position of the missing information.

Example:

```text
Ei yum Sao?
```

**Meaning:** What do I eat?

Here, `Sao` occupies the object position.

Another example:

```text
Sao yum buff?
```

**Meaning:** Who eats meat?

Here, `Sao` occupies the subject position.

This allows information questions to use the normal sentence structure rather than requiring a separate question word order.

---

## Pluralization

The plural marker `di` comes after the word it pluralizes.

Examples:

```text
Ei di
Ao di
Sa di
Nu di
```

These mean:

* we/us
* you all
* they
* they/those things

For nouns:

```text
maimai di
```

**Meaning:** goats.

In a demonstrative noun phrase, `di` follows the noun:

```text
ti maimai di
```

**Meaning:** these goats.

```text
ta maimai di
```

**Meaning:** those goats.

The demonstrative `ti` or `ta` directly modifies the noun, while `di` marks the noun as plural.

---

## Possession and Relationships

Possession and relationships use the marker `de`.

**Structure:**

```text
Possessor + de + Possessed
```

Examples:

```text
Ei de maimai.
```

**Meaning:** my goat.

```text
Ei di de maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** our goats.

The possessor comes before `de`, and the possessed or related noun comes after `de`.

---

## Demonstrative Phrases

Demonstratives directly modify the noun.

**Structure:**

```text
Demonstrative + Noun
```

Examples:

```text
ti maimai
```

**Meaning:** this goat.

```text
ta maimai
```

**Meaning:** that goat.

For plural nouns:

```text
ti maimai di
```

**Meaning:** these goats.

```text
ta maimai di
```

**Meaning:** those goats.

The plural marker does not directly follow `ti` or `ta` in a demonstrative noun phrase.

---

## Combining Structures

Established grammatical elements can be combined while keeping their normal order.

Example:

```text
ta maimai di la Ei di de maimai di.
```

**Meaning:** Those goats are our goats.

Structure:

```text
ta maimai di       la       Ei di de maimai di
Subject            Copula   Complement
```

Another example:

```text
Ei di yum buff ma?
```

**Meaning:** Do we eat meat?

The plural marker remains attached to the pronoun:

```text
Ei di
```

The verb remains unchanged:

```text
yum
```

The question marker remains at the end:

```text
ma?
```

---

## Core Word-Order Rules

1. The basic sentence order is **Subject + Verb + Object**.
2. The copular structure is **Subject + `la` + Complement**.
3. `na` comes before the predicate.
4. `ma` comes at the end of a yes/no question.
5. `Sao` appears where the missing information would normally occur.
6. `di` follows the word it pluralizes.
7. Possession uses **Possessor + `de` + Possessed**.
8. Demonstratives directly modify the noun.
9. In demonstrative noun phrases, `di` follows the noun.
10. Verbs remain unchanged regardless of subject, number, or gender.
11. Existing grammatical markers should be combined in their established positions rather than creating unnecessary alternative word orders.

---

## Summary

Luma Lina uses a predictable word-order system based primarily on SVO.

| Structure            | Pattern                      | Example           |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Basic sentence       | Subject + Verb + Object      | `Ei yum buff.`    |
| Copula               | Subject + `la` + Complement  | `Ei la Adeel.`    |
| Negation             | Subject + `na` + Predicate   | `Ei na yum buff.` |
| Yes/no question      | Sentence + `ma?`             | `Ei yum buff ma?` |
| Information question | `Sao` in missing position    | `Sao yum buff?`   |
| Possession           | Possessor + `de` + Possessed | `Ei de maimai.`   |
| Plural               | Word + `di`                  | `maimai di`       |
| Demonstrative        | Demonstrative + Noun         | `ti maimai`       |
| Demonstrative plural | Demonstrative + Noun + `di`  | `ti maimai di`    |

The goal of word order in Luma Lina is **simple, consistent, compositional, predictable, and expandable**.
