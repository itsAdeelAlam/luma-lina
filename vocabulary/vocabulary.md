# Vocabulary

**Status:** Official

## Purpose

This document contains the current vocabulary of **Luma Lina**.

Only established vocabulary is listed as Official. Proposed or experimental words should be documented separately in the development files until they are accepted.

---

## Personal Pronouns

| Word | Meaning  | Status   |
| ---- | -------- | -------- |
| `Ei` | I / me   | Official |
| `Ao` | you      | Official |
| `Sa` | he / she | Official |
| `Nu` | it       | Official |

Luma Lina does not use grammatical gender for personal pronouns.

`Sa` can refer to a male or female person.

---

## Plural Pronouns

The plural marker `di` follows a pronoun to make it plural.

| Form    | Meaning             | Status   |
| ------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| `Ei di` | we / us             | Official |
| `Ao di` | you all             | Official |
| `Sa di` | they                | Official |
| `Nu di` | they / those things | Official |

The pronoun itself does not change.

## Verbs

| Word  | Meaning | Status   |
| ----- | ------- | -------- |
| `yum` | eat     | Official |

Luma Lina verbs do not change according to person, number, or gender.

Example:

```text
Ei yum buff.
Ao yum buff.
Sa yum buff.
Ei di yum buff.
```

The verb `yum` remains unchanged.

## Plural Vocabulary

`di` is the official plural marker.

It follows the word it pluralizes.

Examples:

```text
maimai di
```

= goats

```text
Ei di
```

= we / us

```text
Ao di
```

= you all

```text
Sa di
```

= they

Pluralization does not require changing the original word.

---

## Possessive Forms

Possession and relationships use the marker `de`.

The basic structure is:

```text
Possessor + de + Possessed
```

Examples:

```text
Ei de maimai
```

= my goat

```text
Ei di de maimai di
```

= our goats

```text
Ao de maimai
```

= your goat

```text
Sa de maimai
```

= his/her goat

```text
Nu de maimai
```

= its goat

The marker `de` does not change according to person or number.

---

## Demonstrative Forms

The demonstratives `ti` and `ta` directly modify the noun.

The plural marker `di` follows the noun.

### Near

```text
ti maimai
```

= this goat

```text
ti maimai di
```

= these goats

### Distant

```text
ta maimai
```

= that goat

```text
ta maimai di
```

= those goats

The plural marker does **not** directly follow `ti` or `ta` in a demonstrative noun phrase.

The correct structure is:

```text
Demonstrative + Noun + di
```

## Question Vocabulary

Yes/no questions use `ma`:

```text
Ei yum buff ma?
```

= Do I eat meat?

Information questions use `Sao`:

```text
Ei yum Sao?
```

= What do I eat?

```text
Sao yum buff?
```

= Who eats meat?

Boolean responses use:

```text
Yah.
```

= Yes.

```text
Na.
```

= No.

`na` is also the general negation marker.

---

## Negation Vocabulary

`na` is the official general negation marker.

Example:

```text
Ei na yum buff.
```

= I do not eat meat.

The same marker can be used in a negative yes/no question:

```text
Ei na yum buff ma?
```

= Don't I eat meat?

---

## Core Vocabulary Principles

1. Vocabulary should be easy to pronounce.
2. Words should be easy to remember.
3. New words should be distinct from existing words.
4. Grammatical functions should preferably use reusable markers.
5. Existing vocabulary should be reused when possible.
6. Unnecessary synonyms should be avoided.
7. Vocabulary should remain simple, consistent, and expandable.
8. New vocabulary should not become Official until explicitly accepted.
